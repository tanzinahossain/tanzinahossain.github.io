Corporate Disclosure, Financial NLP, Evidence Retrieval & Large Language Models

I am an Associate Professor of Finance at Daffodil International University and a PhD candidate in Information Science with a concentration in Data Science at the University of North Texas. My work lies at the intersection of finance, accounting, and information science.

My research examines corporate disclosures, semantic change, information novelty, and evidence retrieval from financial documents. I use natural language processing, machine learning, contextual text embeddings, large language models, and panel-data econometrics to study whether corporate narratives provide meaningful information to investors and other decision-makers.

I bring more than sixteen years of university teaching experience in corporate finance, investments, financial markets, and research methods. My teaching connects financial theory and computational methods with practical business decisions.

I welcome opportunities to collaborate on research involving finance, accounting, data analytics, and emerging AI methods.

Teaching Philosophy

My teaching focuses on a practical question: What should students be able to do by the end of the class? I begin with what students already know, explain new concepts in clear language, and give them opportunities to apply those concepts and defend their reasoning. In finance and quantitative courses, I connect theory with real financial data, business problems, and managerial decisions. I want students not only to perform an analysis, but also to interpret the results, evaluate their limitations, and communicate their conclusions clearly.
